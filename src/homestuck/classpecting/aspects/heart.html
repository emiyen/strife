heart

identity and self are things i've had a contentious relationship with historically, but i think most people have a phase of identity exploration as a part of psychological development. so that's pretty par for the course.*

i've been my own worst enemy for as long as i can remember, yadda yadda. but at the risk of sounding self-absorbed, i'm also fascinated by the concept of identity and the self. i want to know more about myself. i want to view myself from an outsider's perspective. i want to know all of my idiosyncracies and issues from a bird's eye view, if only so that i can make better informed decisions in my life and grow as a person (because people are not static, immutable objects). changing yourself is incredibly hard, but it is possible, and i am a firm believer in that. but it requires self-knowledge, much of which i consider myself to be lacking in.

so in a way, i guess you could call me self-obsessed. i just think that self-knowledge—true self-knowledge—is something a lot of people could do more with, and would lead to a lot of better outcomes in interpersonal issues and in making decisions for oneself. i do admit that i have fascination with selfhood even outside of motivations of personal growth, though. i'm stuck with me for my whole life—why wouldn't i want to know more?

my self-obsession borders on morbid fascination, because i want to know all of the ugly things, too. the only way i can grow is to learn about these parts of myself and acknowledge them.

* a large part of growing up, particularly during your teenage years, is trying to carve out a sense of identity for yourself. it's all about unstable identity and trying to figure out what and who you are, at least for a lot of people. that's how it was for me. i swear there was a term for this in psychology but it evades me and my ass is too lazy to go searching through my notes for the answer. you know how it is. chances are you've either already gone through it or are going through it right now.
