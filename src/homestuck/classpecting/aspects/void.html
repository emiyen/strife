void

would've been fun to make this page lead to nowhere but i want to put some actual thought into this. so i'm sacrificing the bit.

this one's a bit hard for me to connect to myself and my life. it's a very abstract aspect.

i don't have much to say about meaninglessness/lack of relevance. i don't care for relevance and much prefer to stay out of the spotlight, personally. being known and seen from time to time is a nice feeling, but i wouldn't want it to be frequent or constant, and i definitely wouldn't want to be the center of attention.

things like this site are what i consider "safe" ways to reveal parts of myself. i control what goes out, and no one is obligated to look at it or respond to it. it just exists in my very small corner of the web; i get to express myself without the terrifying feeling of being Known. i'm still being vulnerable to a degree, but i don't really have to deal with the consequences of that vulnerability.

i also have no qualms with the idea of things being "meaningless", because i think meaning is ascribed to things by you, not some outside, higher force.

secrets are a different thing. the existence of a secret implies a lie by omission, direct obfuscation of existing knowledge instead of a simple lack of it.

i wouldn't say i keep many secrets. for most things, i'm open and honest if you ask me a direct question. i often feel like i'm transparent to those who look at me—that i'm an open book, albeit unintentionally. i am, however, prone to what some would consider lies of omission, which i'm sure falls under the domain of void. there are simply things that other people are not entitled to know about me, even if that knowledge may change things.

there's safety in obfuscation and irrelevance. i love to fly under the radar. one thing i enjoy doing sometimes is simply lurking in discord servers and watching people interact, for example, as creepy as that may sound. an excerpt from one of my blog posts about this:

there's something so appealing and comfortable about being able to be completely invisible but still being able to "interact" with others with my technical presence. there are no expectations for me to react or respond. i get my fill of "social interaction". i can drop in at random if i feel and spark up a conversation. it makes for a comfortable social environment for me compared to in-person interactions.

it feels like a "safe" way to interact with others and fulfill my social needs. the distance is important to my sense of security.

as i type this, i'm noticing a general trend of security/safety when it comes to my relationship with void. something to ponder on, i suppose.
