Classpecting Questionnaire

original questionnaire post can be found here. credits to rosespecting on tumblr for the questions.

note: i was high as hell when i initially answered this questionnaire. additional comments will be made in #f2a400 orange to help differentiate when i was high and when i was sober/revising.

What do you focus on the most: the past, the present, or the future? Why?

i'd say the future, but my real answer is i have no idea. i avoid thinking about the past because there's no point unless i'm learning from it, and i avoid thinking about the future to the best of my ability because it brings me dread and stress. and i'm not very in tune with the present. my ass is detached from the whole of my own existence

if i truly had to choose, i'd say the future. it's the thing i consider most important to keep in mind even though i find it stressful as hell. i'm always in a state of trying to prepare myself for the future mentally.

What do you do when you have nothing to do? What would you do if you couldn't even do that?

sometimes i just lurk in online public spaces and watch conversations happen. it's interesting and entertaining sometimes. or i draw. or i busy myself with something else that can keep my mind from wandering into places i don't like.

How well do you know yourself? How do you determine what is or is not part of your self?

i know myself better than i did when i was a teenager, but that's not saying much, because i didn't know myself at all as a teen.

i view things as a part of myself if they've become established patterns and routines i've fallen into, but i also think the self is capable of drastic change. shoutout neuroplasticity.

i value external opinions when it comes to myself, as i'm of the opinion that self is a blind spot for me. i don't trust my own judgement about myself a lot of the time* and prefer to gather data on what other people think about me in order to form my own conclusion from multiple other perspectives.

* my sense of identity has gotten stronger over the years, so i can say i'm not as deferential as i used to be about accepting views of myself from other people as my actual identity. if someone states an opinion on how i behave that sounds ridiculous, i have more confidence in my ability to say that i'm not like that.

How do you make hard decisions? What kind of advice do you tend to take to heart?

i go to friends for external input on my decisions and gauge how i feel about what i should do based on the following: my reactions to their suggestions, and combining multiple perspectives with mine to give me a better picture of what i want to make a decision on.

i try to take the advice i want to hear the least to heart. if i'm having an instinctive reaction to it, there's probably something lying underneath that reaction that should be explored.

How naturally does skepticism come to you? If you manually work to be more or less skeptical, how do you go about that?

i'm not skeptical enough, i don't think. i've historically had the habit of simply going along with what my social environment tells me is okay to do, which doesn't bode well in unhealthy spaces. i don't question myself enough, not in the ways that are important and targeted/directed. i certainly don't question others enough. i'm too busy trying to survive most social interactions until i can leave them.

social anxiety's a bitch.

How do you react to being challenged on your opinions? How do you wish you acted?

it depends on how strong my opinion is. i try to keep myself open to any and every perspective. i actually wish i felt more strongly about things and had more strong opinions. i'm so neutral and disconnected from what i feel about things that i often turn my brain off and led others lead. it stems from not truating myself and my judgement at all, to the point of deferring to outside sources. it's also often just easier than potentially going against the grain.

if a belief of mine is questioned, i immediately start trying to examine myself and try to figure out what i got wrong/if i am wrong. i have little confidence in a lot of my own takes because i always feel like i don't have enough information.

How easy is it for you to communicate with your friends? How about when you're angry with them?

it's difficult for me to communicate, but it's something i'm working on. if i feel like i'm angry at a friend for an understandable and justified reason, though, i've been known to make myself clear. it's only when i doubt myself or find my emotions illogical/unjustified that i have trouble communicating, which is semi-frequently.

What's more uncomfortable for you, when everything is changing or when nothing is? How do you respond to this discomfort?

it's more stressful and uncomfortable when everything is changing. i do badly in fast-paced, constantly changing environments. i often need time to decompress and recollect myself. i'm very much a creature of comfort.

i often go retreating back into my comfort zones where things are familiar and safe.

What do you do when a problem seems insurmountable? How would you feel if you later found out that it truly was impossible to solve?

i give up a little too easily. or at least, i'm constantly fighting the desire to give up. it's hard to wrestle with.

finding out that a task or problem was truly impossible would be relieving for me, because it would mean my failure isn't my fault. but i believe most problems have a solution/can be surmounted—it's just that i don't know those solutions.

How easily do you get overwhelmed? How do you bounce back, and how long does it take you?

i get overwhelmed easily, and i need a good amount of time to piece myself back together after i've been rattled. but i also know i tend to focus too hard on my limitations and what i can't do, trying to give myself a lot of padding and room to operate in in case i'm bad at something or fail. i undersell myself to make things easier for myself when it comes to challenges. there's a chance i could recover quickly—i'm just afraid to try without training wheels on.

What does legacy mean to you? How much do you value it?

legacy doesn't mean very much to me personally. i know it means a lot to other people, though, and i think that's normal. i just don't find a lot of value in it myself.

i guess my favorite form of legacy would be to know you passed on wisdom and knowledge to someone else and helped to make their life better somehow. and the knowledge that that knowledge will be further passed down is nice.

When the ending of a story leaves you a bit confused, what do you do? If this story were written by a friend looking for advice, what would you say to them?

i sit in my confusion for a bit. sometimes i just accept it. other times i might go looking for the opinions of other people on the matter to see what they think. then i form my own opinion after comparing and contrasting other opinions and what i like or don't like about them. but i'm comfortable accepting my lack of knowledge or understanding on some things sometimes.

if it were written by a friend looking for advice, i'd outline the things i'm confused about so they can see if whatever they wanted to accomplish or evoke with their writing was ineffective or not. what did they want me to leave feeling? what did they intend for me to know? that way i can fill in some blind spots in their writing.

How easily do you fit in with those around you? How hard are you willing to try?

i have the feeling i don't fit in easily, but that could just be self-consciousness. i think on the outside i appear to function fine regarding fitting in—passably, at least—but if you look a little closer you can tell i'm uncomfortable and want to leave. i don't care for fitting in socially as much as more image conscious people might be, and i'd rather just escape the situation if i feel like i'm sticking out like a sore thumb. i hate most forms of attention when focused on me.

When someone is struggling, do you find it easier to solve their problem yourself or to guide them through it? Which method do you prefer when people are helping you?

i want to guide people through things rather than solving their problems for them. i think it's better for everyone in the long term, and it's best to foster independence in people by giving them the tools to do things for themselves. (it also lessens the burden on me.)

i prefer the same treatment, even though i know it's easier in the short-term to have other people solve things for me. in an ideal world, i'd be fully competent and completely independent, knowing how to handle things all on my own and having the competency to do so.

Do you feel like your problems more often come from within or without? If someone blames you for something you don't think you had any part in, how do you respond?

they absolutely come from within. i am my own worst enemy, yadda yadda. i feel like i'm always holding myself back by focusing too hard on my limitations. i have a bit of a scarcity mindset relative to my energy and capabilities. i know the issues i face in life are largely self-created and self-perpetuated.

if i'm blamed for something i absolutely don't think i had any part in, i'm defending myself or shutting down. i don't care for false accusations and being forced to shoulder responsibility that isn't mine to bear. so i'll either defend myself if i care enough about the other person to do so, or i'll just leave.

How high are your standards for yourself? How much do those standards leak into your opinion of those around you, if at all?

i think my standards for myself can be high when it comes to matters of social norms and following unspoken social rules, or adhering to keeping the collective peace. it's something i've grown more aware of because sometimes my disdain leaks out for others, and then i realize i'm being unfair because of how i view the obligation to control oneself and limit burdening others.

i try not to have expectations for people, though, and i keep my expectations for myself low if i'm not confident in order avoid disappointing myself. there are just some things that i adhere to strictly socially that feel like sacrilege to break.

to clarify more on this: i get very jarred seeing people who act more vulnerably and earnestly in public settings, or people who are openly, obviously insecure. it kind of scares me and makes me wonder, am i that transparent too? i have a lot of expectations for myself about limiting how much of an emotional weight i put on others, including things that could possibly prompt people to feel like they need to comfort me.

Are you a fast learner? If you have a strong natural talent for something, does it urge you to get better, or are you more likely to put that effort elsewhere?

i like to think i learn quickly. strong natural talent pushes me towards things, but i do enjoy some mental challenge as well.. art, for example, took me many years to get better at, and i still consider myself a beginner—it's a constant, neverending journey.

my tolerance for challenge has lowered since i first started to learn how to draw, however. these days i'm more inclined to stick with what i'm already good at. things i've already put the work into.

one exception i have to this is coding. i've been really enjoying teaching myself html/css in the patchwork manner i've been going about it. instead of learning the fundamentals, i'm bouncing all over the place and only learning what i immediately need at the moment to do what i want to do.

it's a surprisingly fun process. i love being forced to find the solution myself. it's a rare moment where being stumped by questions or problems doesn't make me feel incompetent. there's almost always an answer somewhere out there—i just need to persevere.

Do you think people expect too much of you? Do you think you expect too much of others?

i always feel like people expect too much of me, but i'm aware a lot of that is me projecting my fears outward and imagining what i think people expect of me. i just hate it when people have positive expectations of me. it feels like i'm slated to fail.

my expectations of others are pretty basic, i like to imagine. be relatively respectful, mind your business, don't try to control me. but i know my expectations for what people should do with their emotions can sometimes be unreasonable or cold. see: the question about standards

When imagining an ideal future, do you try to build out from where you are now, or do you try to build backwards from the endpoint? How well can you do the opposite?

i try to build out from where i am now. i want to be realistic about things. i'm not so good at working backwards from an ideal endpoint because my imagination is pretty poor when it comes to positive, hopeful/idealistic things.

i really struggle with envisioning a future for myself. it's something relatively new to me, the idea of having a future. it's all quite murky even when i try to build out from where i am now, because nothing is certain.

Lastly and most importantly, do you make your bed? Why or why not?

i do not make my bed. it's going to be a mess later anyway once i sleep in it again, so i don't see much of a point.
