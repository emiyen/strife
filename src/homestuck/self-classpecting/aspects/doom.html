doom

fate/futility

i don't believe in the idea of fate. i think it would be somewhat comforting to believe in predestination of some kind, but i simply... don't. i think we're all in this in a huge web of chaos called life, and the clashes between peoples' wills and choices are what create outcomes, not a prewritten fate. there is no cyclical grand scheme. just a bunch of living beings trying to exist.

futility, on the other hand, i have a slightly different relationship with.

i'm of the general opinion that most if not all things can be changed; there is no outcome that is unavoidable, except perhaps death and the march towards death. but the events in our life, the outcomes? these things are not immutable.

here's the problem: i don't believe in my ability to create meaningful change in my life. i feel like my life is a constant fight against the desire to give up and give in to whatever outcome awaits me. i'm learning to fight harder against it, but it's pretty difficult. in the back of my head, there's always a voice that's saying "there's no use in trying."

rules/restrictions

this is probably the part of doom that has the most relevance to me and my life, though i don't know how doom-related my definition and experience with "rules" are.

i'm always looking out for what the "rules" are in any given situation. the easiest example to give is in social scenarios; there are always rules, often unspoken, that people have to follow lest they be ostracized. the rules will differ from group to group, from culture to culture, so you have to adapt to them. it applies to broader situations as well: there are rules and expectations for what you're supposed to do in a workplace environment, for example. in any case, in a new situation or environment, i'll have to learn what these "rules" are and adjust my routine to them if needed (or decide if i like them in the first place). i often move alongside these rules and restrictions in the hopes of lessening the friction in my life, meaning i often take the path of least resistance.

the thing is, i don't always adhere faithfully and strictly to these rules. my favorite thing is to be able to avoid them altogether by simply not being in the situation/escaping it. that way i don't have to deal with all of the rules and expectations. i essentially do my best to fly under the radar so i can have the freedoms to do as i please. tldr; i'd rather not participate in the system at all than have to navigate ten gorbillion rules.

but they're something i'm always aware of, and where there are rules and a situation i have to or want to be in, i'll typically fall under the category of people who follow them, at the very least the bare minimum in order to get by.

sacrifice

i don't have much of a relationship with sacrifice. i consider myself a pretty stingy person. i try to limit what i give out to people, in both literal and emotional terms. i avoid sacrificing myself, my time, my energy, unless i truly feel like it's worth it, and that i can afford it. and i often feel like i can't afford it due to a bit of a scarcity mindset i have.
