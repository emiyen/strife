--- date: "2026-10-11T15:13:00" title: "reflecting on my struggles with introjection/merging" tags: - personal - self-reflection - introjection - enneagram - e9 ---

something akin to introjection as a self-stabilizing defense mechanism is something that i've struggled with for a long time. in fact, i'm still struggling with it to this day. working on this part of myself is very much still something that is in-progress.

as children, we tend to introject the beliefs and values of our parents, guardiants, authority figures, etc. it's how we learn about the world, absorbing beliefs like a sponge to create a map that we can later use to navigate the chaos around us in the absence of guiding figures. it's a normal part of psychological development. when we're younger, we don't question the beliefs—we simply absorb them and turn them into our own. it become a problem when we either introject maladaptive beliefs and values, or we continue to introject compulsively beyond our childhood and teen years. i struggle the most with the latter (though i am not exempt from the former). i believe an accurate parallel to this behavior of mine, if not an outright description of it, would be what the enneagram type 9 does.

to quote cpenneagram:

Over-Adjustment and Merging: The prominent Type Nine habit of adapting themselves to others’ agendas represents an “over-adjustment” because Nines typically don’t just meet others halfway, they lose the ability to access what their positions are and give in to others completely—often without anyone knowing they’ve done it.

and to quote a user on the r/enneagram subreddit:

Nines are a body type and want autonomy: the right to occupy space and follow their own rhythm. But instead of actively claiming it (like an Eight would), they secure that right by "disappearing".

They withdraw into an inner sanctuary, a soft, conflict-free world where everything is already okay and nothing can affect them. Outwardly this reads as becoming agreeable or passive. Yet the agreeableness conceals a quiet refusal, saying "no" without saying so.

The cost is that autonomy survives only in its weakest form: a right to be undisturbed rather than embodied agency.

This is pretty much what numbing out means. Imagine this like a volume wheel for life. Nines turn the volume down just enough to be unbothered. Obviously this happens unconsciously, but especially in times of stress or pain we can dial it down quite a notch to not deal with it.

On the behaviour level this is supported by rumination (thinking about stuff, fantasizing, without landing on a clear "solution", direction or action to take), distracting oneself with low-level tasks and activities (binge-watching, eating, going along with the agendas of other people, submerging oneself in supportive functions, etc.).

thinking for myself is a struggle when i'm in the presence of other people, especially when i perceive them as having stronger wills than myself. around others, i'm often in a state driven by anxiety where i try to attune to the feelings and beliefs of others, taking them on as my own to avoid friction and conflict. it's simply "safer" to go along with the masses and not question what i really want or feel.

in truth, i often don't know what i want or feel, outside of the simple desire to be undisturbed. i am so incredibly out of touch with myself that it's easier to take on what others believe than to dig deep into what's going on with me internally. i've had several instances where i've found myself swept up in the energy and feelings of social circles i've been a part of, only to find myself regretting it deeply later or questioning whether i really believed in anything i was doing or saying, and with no one to blame but myself.

when i can't feel out what another person's views are (rather: what i think they want me to feel) my anxiety grows. i don't know how i'm supposed to align myself. a part of me fears that there is nothing underneath: that i am an empty person to be filled with the whims and desires of other people.

a lot of this struggle is why in the present day i have become more isolated by my own hand (though it isn't the only reason). i used to gravitate towards people with strong convictions—or at least, the people who are the loudest about them—but i've realized that that only serves to blind myself further. there are, of course, times where i find myself disagreeing with people on matters of principle. i don't always go along with other peoples' agendas. but more often than not, i allow myself to simply stop thinking and numb out.

i'm an adult now, however, at the big age of 23. it's no longer something i should do, introjecting the people around me's beliefs about anything and everything. merging with people's agendas in order to feel connected without having my peace disturbed. in a way, it puts the responsibility onto other people. "if i was simply following what other people were doing, then it's not my fault if that thing was bad, right? because i didn't truly believe it." i fear having incorrect thoughts and beliefs, so it's so much easier to adopt those of others than to form an original thought.

as you can imagine, this isn't something that i'm proud of. it's a point of shame. i've always found people with their own convictions to be fascinating because of the fact that i never felt like i had my own—because how, really, do you trust yourself enough to do so? how do you not question everything you start to think or say because it could be "wrong?" that's what i could never understand about 1s and 8s, the other types in the anger/gut triad. they seem to have a concrete trust in themself and their ideals that i simply do not.

i have no clue how to get started in breaking out of this behavior pattern. my band-aid solution has just been to make my social circles extremely small and selective on top of trying to remain aware of when i start to introject other peoples' opinions so i can take a step back, but it happens so automatically that sometimes i fail to catch it. i'm steadily getting better at becoming more aware of it, though. baby steps.

one day i'll reach a point where i'm truly in tune with my own opinions. for now, i'll just have to work at it.
