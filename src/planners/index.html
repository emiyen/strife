my planner journey

-- the beginning

i got my first hobonichi cousin, an A5 sized planner with a variety of spreads, on september 10th, 2025. i'd developed a fixation on planning and journaling, and after watching many videos on youtube, i decided to get one for myself. it seemed like a good fit for my needs.

since it was way past the beginning of the year, there were many pages leftover in the front of the planner. but before moving to analog journaling i'd been (in)frequently journaling in obsidian, a markdown editor. i proceeded to backfill the pages of my hobonichi with my digital journal entries so i could have everything in one place.

-- layout and flow

from then on, the planner was a huge hit for me. the layout i used (and still use today) is based on little coco studio's layout from this video. every day on the left side of the page i write out what's scheduled/planned for the day and the tasks i had to do. on the right side, i journal. this simple and functional layout has been the one i've been using ever since, and the only deviations i've made have been what i write the headings with (i've since switched from a felt brush pen to just using my fountain pen for the entire page) and getting rid of the habits section that i'd tried out for a bit.

i didn't bother with washi tape, stickers, or any other decorations that i saw many youtubers using. i knew that if i complicated things, i would be more likely to view using my planner as a chore and avoid it, particularly on low-energy days. keeping things simple and relatively frictionless has helped with my planner use consistency greatly.

there were, of course, some days where i couldn't be bothered, or days where i was so fixated on other things that i put planning aside. but overall i've seen huge improvements in my quality of life with the introduction of a planner into my life.

-- benefits to using a planner/journal

before my hobonichi, i really struggled with remembering when things happened in my life. days blurred together even when major events occurred because i never took note of the dates they happened. my brain simpy didn't register dates as important. i'd fail to remember dates for my doctor, my psych, my therapist, and so on, when attempting to recount things.

while my memory hasn't exactly improved drastically since i started using it, it's very easy for me to bring my planner to refer to when i need to. now i can actually tell you how my day, week, month, or year was, instead of blanking out entirely.

memorykeeping has also been a bonus. i never thought of myself as someone who would enjoy looking back on past events, but recording things and being able to see them after the fact has been surprisingly comforting, and helps remind me of good things that have happened in my life. it's fascinating, reading things i wrote and actually being able to recall writing them and the way i was feeling at the time.

finally, the journaling has helped clear my head on numerous occasions. it's a fantastic outlet for turbulent emotions, and i really do feel like people would benefit from it.

how do i get started?

-- choosing a planner

there's no need to shill out on getting the more expensive options like i did if you're unsure this is something you'll actually keep up with. you can probably find decent enough planners at target for the sake of exploration.

if you're dead set on getting a higher quality planner, or specifically want planners with paper that is fountain pen friendly, here are some popular brand options:

  • aura estelle
  • hobonichi (jetpens / official shop)
  • sterling ink
  • wonderland 222

before you go dropping money on any of these, i highly recommend going onto youtube and checking out flip-throughs of these planners and looking over the spreads within (most sites also have previews of the inside), as well as the available sizes. ask yourself if you prefer more writing space or ease in portability. -- choosing a writing utensil

start out with your favorite pen or pencil if you have one. any will do, though if you got a planner with fountain pen friendly paper, i'd of course recommend a fountain pen to get the most bang for your buck.

-- choosing a layout

start out simple before you start to experiment with more complex layouts that require more effort. the spread i talked about earlier—events and tasks on the left, journaling on the right—has worked well for me, but you'll want to tailor the layout you choose to your needs.

maybe you want to do bullet journaling. maybe you don't want to journal at all and would prefer to use the daily pages for lists or as scratch paper. maybe you want to do a doodle every day. if you want to build a habit of using your planner, you want to put function first before adding any bells and whistles. worrying about it looking pretty will probably detract from your experience unless you're getting into planners with the express purpose of making it look aesthetically pleasing and find that to be enjoyable.
